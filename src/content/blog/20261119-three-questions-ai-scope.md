## TL;DR

- McKinsey's 2026 survey reports regular AI use in at least one business function by nearly nine in ten respondents.
- The same survey finds that 37% attribute at least some EBIT impact to AI use, about the same share as last year.
- Three answers set a project's scope before anyone chooses a model: the decision, the baseline and the owner. None of them depends on which model you pick.

## What the survey shows

McKinsey's report, *The state of AI in 2026: On the road to ROI*, draws on an online survey that ran from May 4 to June 8, 2026, with 1,719 participants in 97 nations.

Use is widespread. Nearly nine in ten respondents report regular use of AI in at least one business function, and 44 percent now say AI is scaling across their enterprise, up from 38 percent a year ago.

Reported financial impact is narrower. The report says 37 percent of respondents "attribute at least some EBIT impact to AI use," about the same share as last year. The report describes the figure as a share of respondents. The share of AI high performers, respondents who attribute at least 5 percent of EBIT to AI and call its impact "significant," "has remained flat at about 6 percent of all respondents."

## Three questions

1. The decision: Which decision will the project change, how often is it made, and what is a better answer worth?
2. The baseline: What number will move, what is it today, and what result counts as success?
3. The owner: Which named person signs off on the result after launch, and answers when the output is wrong?

## An example

Take a regional lender that wants a tool to pre-fill loan files. Nobody has timed how long a file takes today, and the credit manager expects reviewers to check every field anyway. The project starts with a tool and no number to beat, so after three months nobody can say whether it helped.

This scenario is illustrative. It is not a client.
